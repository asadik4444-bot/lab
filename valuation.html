How this model works

Ten annual free cash flows to the firm, discounted at the company WACC. Without annual inputs, the model generates the ten years from your assumptions: growth is constant in years 1–5, then fades to terminal growth by year 10, and the EBIT margin moves in a straight line to its year-10 level. With the capex rule on, capex above depreciation counts as growth investment and returns to the depreciation level in years 6–10. With the rule off, the capex ratio holds for all ten years. The capex seed is bounded to depreciation plus revenue growth; a negative growth seed counts as zero. With annual inputs, the ten rows replace the generated years. Terminal reinvestment equals growth divided by return on capital. With the default setting, return on capital equals WACC: investment after year 10 earns only its cost and creates no extra value. Terminal growth still sets the profit of year 11.

The same claims and current-share bridge feeds each view. Multiple bands are selected inputs, not peer research. The reverse calculation holds all other inputs fixed and solves the first-five-year growth, or the discount rate, that gives the entered price. Annual overrides disable the generated cases and the reverse growth solve.

Year-end discounting. Tax applies to positive EBIT. No tax-loss carryforward. Review the earnings period, share class, cash needs, lease treatment and financing assumptions.
